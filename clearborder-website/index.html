    Built for the layer of customs nobody serves small firms in.

    UK customs compliance has settled into six layers. HMRC is digitising the systems and publishing the content for free. Brokers carry the liability. The judgement in between, deciding what a product is and where it qualifies as coming from, is where SMEs have no affordable option.

      Not a broker

      Tariffmark never files on its own authority or takes on the declarant's liability. It prepares the decision; your team or your representative approves and files.

      Not a lookup tool

      HMRC's tools explain what a code means. Tariffmark works out which code is yours, tests origin, and produces the evidence pack.

      The decision-and-evidence layer

      A defensible determination for UK SME trade, built to the British Standard for customs intermediaries and priced for firms that will never hire a customs specialist.

      From a shipping document to an approved determination.

      The planned first session takes one real document and produces a decision you can act on. The aim is under 30 minutes for a first determination and under five for a repeat product once it is in your catalogue.

      1. Upload what you have

        A commercial invoice, packing list or product specification. No customs knowledge needed to start.

      2. Extract the facts

        Material, form, function, value, origin of components and destination are pulled into structured product attributes.

      3. Classify by the rules

        A fixed sequence: chapter and heading candidates, section and chapter notes, exclusions, the General Interpretative Rules, then subheadings.

      4. Test origin and duty

        Your bill of materials is checked against the agreement's product-specific rule. You see the verdict, the proof to keep, and the duty position.

      5. You decide

        Approve, add information, or send for expert review. Low-confidence or high-exposure cases are routed to a person automatically.

      6. Export and keep

        Customs-ready paperwork plus an evidence record pinned to the rules in force that day, retained for six years.

      A person signs off every consequential decision.

      Published studies show human and AI teams beat either alone when the human is the stronger party, and human experts still classify goods far more accurately than autonomous AI agents. So Tariffmark does the research, narrowing and rule-checking, and leaves the call with you. You or your representative remain the legal declarant.

      What Tariffmark will never say

      • "HMRC-approved." HMRC does not endorse customs software. The CDS developer list is a directory, not an accreditation.
      • An accuracy figure without its method. Accuracy will be published against a named benchmark, with the method attached.
      • "Guaranteed" duty savings. Savings depend on your goods, your suppliers' evidence and the agreement's rules.
      • That it replaces the customs expert. Experts review the hard cases, and every decision has a named approver.

      Questions traders ask first.

      Does Tariffmark file my customs declaration?

      No. Tariffmark prepares the determination and customs-ready paperwork. You, your forwarder or your customs agent approve it and file it, and remain the declarant. CDS-ready data export is planned for 2028, after integration through HMRC's Developer Hub.

      Is Tariffmark approved by HMRC?

      HMRC does not approve or endorse customs software, and Tariffmark will never claim otherwise. The product is being designed to the British Standard for customs intermediaries, PAS 41201:2026, so that its audit trail fits the requirements intermediaries already face.

      What happens when the AI is not sure?

      Every determination carries a confidence score and a groundedness score. If either falls below your threshold, or the duty at stake is high, the case is routed to review: you can add information, ask an accredited customs practitioner, or build a dossier for an advance tariff ruling from HMRC.

      What if the tariff or an agreement changes?

      Every rule is stored with the dates it applied, and each determination is pinned to the versions it relied on. When a rule changes, the products in your catalogue that it affects are re-checked and you are told what changed and why. The target is within 24 hours of the change.

      Can I use it today?

      Not yet. Tariffmark is in its validation phase and plans its first commercial release in 2027. Customs intermediaries can apply to be one of three to five design partners, and SME traders can join discovery interviews that shape the product and its pricing.

      How long are records kept?

      Six years, matching the record-keeping period PAS 41201 sets for intermediaries. Records stay reproducible: a determination made in 2026 can be shown under the rules in force in 2026 during an audit years later.
